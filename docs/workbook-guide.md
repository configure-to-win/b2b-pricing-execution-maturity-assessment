# Workbook guide

## Before you begin

Choose a clear assessment scope and involve people who understand the current quote process. Depending on the organisation, that may include sales, pricing, finance, commercial operations, deal desk or management.

The assessment is most useful when answers are supported by examples or evidence. It is not a test in which a higher answer is automatically better.

## 1. Start here

Review the purpose, exclusions, response scale and official links. Confirm the workbook version before comparing results from different sessions.

## 2. Quick assessment

Complete the twelve **Current state** dropdowns.

For each statement:

1. read the wording literally;
2. select the response that best describes the defined scope today;
3. add evidence or notes where the answer could otherwise be disputed;
4. leave **Target state** blank unless a future state has been discussed and agreed.

Do not enter an aspirational answer as current state. Do not enter Intelligent merely because continuous improvement would be desirable.

### Useful evidence

- a current source or ownership register;
- recent quote examples;
- effective-date or agreement history;
- calculation specifications and reconciliations;
- override and exception logs;
- approval rules and decision records;
- recurring exception, leakage or control reports.

The assessment status remains incomplete until all twelve current-state questions are answered.

## 3. Maturity profile

Review one row per dimension:

- current average and maturity level;
- optional target average and level;
- gap;
- priority rank;
- recommended next control.

The chart compares current and target states only where both are available. It does not contain a benchmark or total score.

The two highlighted priorities appear only after all current-state questions are complete. Treat them as a starting point for discussion, not an automatic investment decision.

## 4. Maturity matrix

Use the matrix to understand how practices normally change from manual and person-dependent to governed and evidence-led.

The five columns are descriptions, not mandatory stages. A team may deliberately operate at different levels across dimensions. The economically appropriate target depends on quote complexity, risk, volume and control requirements.

Use the evidence column to identify what could substantiate the selected level.

## 5. Improvement priorities

The register starts with one row per dimension and allows additional actions.

For each accepted improvement:

- refine the formula-driven recommended control into a specific action;
- assign an accountable owner;
- choose a realistic target date;
- set a status;
- identify the evidence that will demonstrate completion;
- record dependencies and assumptions in notes.

A maturity gap alone does not prove that an action has positive business value. Consider risk, frequency, effort, dependency and expected operational impact.

## 6. Quote Performance bridge

Review how pricing execution may affect:

- Product & Price Data;
- Currency Control;
- Quote Accuracy;
- Commercial Control;
- True Deal Economics;
- Rebate Impact;
- Approval Logic;
- Quote Cycle Time;
- Delay Cause;
- Quote Release Efficiency;
- Commercial Visibility;
- Error Cause.

These are conceptual relationships, not a score mapping. Complete the full Quote Performance Scan when you need a broader benchmark of the complete quote process.

## 7. Methodology

Use this sheet to audit:

- response mapping;
- dimension formulas;
- thresholds;
- gap calculations;
- blank handling;
- priority ranking;
- limitations and version information.

## 8. Definitions

Use the controlled definitions when team members use the same term differently. Adapt local terminology in your implementation documents without changing the published assessment questions casually.

## 9. Worked example

The fictional industrial-equipment distributor demonstrates all twelve answers, dimension profiles, target gaps, priorities and example actions.

It is not customer data, benchmark data, advice or a recommended policy. Do not copy its targets without assessing your own process.

## Saving and sharing results

The downloaded workbook is local. Using it does not by itself submit entered data to Configure to WIN.

Before sharing a completed copy:

- remove confidential customer or price data that is not necessary;
- confirm the scope and assessment date are clear;
- preserve the workbook version;
- distinguish factual evidence from opinion;
- record who approved improvement actions separately if required by your governance process.
