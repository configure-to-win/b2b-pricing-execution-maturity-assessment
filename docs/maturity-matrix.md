# Pricing execution maturity matrix

Use this matrix to discuss how each pricing practice operates today and what a realistic next control could look like. The levels are descriptions, not universal targets.

## Pricing data foundation

| Level | Process practice |
| --- | --- |
| Reactive | List, cost, floor and currency values are gathered manually from scattered sources. The correct source and version depend on individual knowledge. |
| Emerging | Some sources and owners are identified, but teams still use local copies, incomplete records or manual version checks. |
| Established | Source definitions and update responsibilities are documented and repeatable, but effective dates, freshness or quote-time enforcement remain inconsistent. |
| Advanced | Governed sources are resolved systematically with ownership, effective dates, currency treatment and traceable selected values. |
| Intelligent | Source freshness, usage and failures are measured; drift and stale records are reviewed and controls are improved using evidence. |

Example evidence: controlled price-source register, source owner, effective-date history, currency rule, freshness report and sample quote trace.

## Customer-specific and contracted pricing

| Level | Process practice |
| --- | --- |
| Reactive | Agreements are stored in emails, spreadsheets or previous quotes and must be found and interpreted manually. |
| Emerging | Selected agreements are recorded, but coverage, scope, effective dates or use in quoting remain incomplete or locally managed. |
| Established | Agreement records, scope and validity are documented and used repeatably, but application and deviation control are not consistently enforced. |
| Advanced | The applicable account- and product-specific price is resolved in the quote context; deviations and overrides are calculated, governed and traceable. |
| Intelligent | Agreement quality, expiry, deviation and override patterns are reviewed and used to improve maintenance and execution controls. |

Example evidence: contracted-price record, account/product scope, effective dates, approval source, deviation calculation and override log.

## Calculations and deal economics

| Level | Process practice |
| --- | --- |
| Reactive | Discounts, margins, rebates and incentives are calculated in person-owned spreadsheets or with inconsistent definitions. |
| Emerging | Shared templates or common formulas exist for some quotes, but manual exceptions and competing definitions remain. |
| Established | Calculation definitions are documented and repeatable, but they are not enforced consistently across teams, quote types or systems. |
| Advanced | Governed formulas use traceable inputs to calculate discounts, margins, rebates, incentives and other deal measures systematically. |
| Intelligent | Calculation outcomes, exceptions and formula performance are reconciled and reviewed; definitions are refined through a managed feedback loop. |

Example evidence: calculation specification, formula version, source mapping, reconciliation report and sample calculation trace.

## Price execution

| Level | Process practice |
| --- | --- |
| Reactive | The applied price is selected or overridden manually, with limited visibility into why it was used. |
| Emerging | Pricing guidance exists, but adoption is local and manual overrides are not recorded consistently. |
| Established | Standard price-selection and override practices are documented and repeatable, but enforcement differs by team or workflow. |
| Advanced | The applicable price is applied systematically; overrides require defined context and remain visible, controlled and traceable. |
| Intelligent | Execution accuracy, override patterns and leakage are measured and used to refine price-selection and exception controls. |

Example evidence: quote samples, price-resolution trace, override log, reason codes, release check and exception report.

## Exception and approval governance

| Level | Process practice |
| --- | --- |
| Reactive | Approval depends on judgement, email and individual availability; triggers and authority are unclear. |
| Emerging | Some thresholds and roles are defined, but routing and application vary by team, deal or reviewer. |
| Established | Approval policy, authority and routing are documented and repeatable, but enforcement, evidence or reapproval remains inconsistent. |
| Advanced | Governed rules apply authority, routing and reapproval systematically and preserve the decision context and outcome. |
| Intelligent | Approval outcomes, timing, false positives, exceptions and rule drift are measured and used to refine controlled routing. |

Example evidence: approval policy, approval matrix, rule version, authority record, workflow log, rationale and reapproval test.

## Traceability and performance visibility

| Level | Process practice |
| --- | --- |
| Reactive | Source values, calculations, rules and decisions cannot be reconstructed reliably; reporting is manual and retrospective. |
| Emerging | Some evidence is retained, but source, calculation, approval and reporting records remain fragmented. |
| Established | Required evidence and reporting definitions are documented and repeatable, but traces or management views are incomplete. |
| Advanced | A connected decision trace links inputs, calculations, rules and approvals, while recurring pricing exceptions are visible to management. |
| Intelligent | Exception, leakage and control-gap trends are monitored; completed improvements and emerging drift are reviewed using evidence. |

Example evidence: decision trace, exception report, dashboard definition, audit sample, leakage analysis and improvement log.

## Selecting a target

Select the level required by the quote population and control context. Intelligent is not automatically the correct target. A stable, low-risk process may be adequately controlled at a lower level, while a high-volume or exception-heavy process may justify more systematic measurement.
